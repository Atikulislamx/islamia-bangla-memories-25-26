The library does not announce itself. Its best hours are the ones nobody
competes for: early afternoon, when the building is warm, the fans are loud and
the reading room is nearly empty.

## What a memory can leave out

Not every memory needs a photograph. Some are better with a sentence, a date
and nothing else — especially when the point is the atmosphere rather than the
people in the room.

That is why entries in this archive are allowed to be text-only, and why the
archive grid falls back to a typographic card when there is no image.

## Why the small details matter

A list of programmes and dates is a record. It is not a memory. What makes the
archive worth reading years from now is the detail that no official document
would ever include: which seat was always taken, which page of a book was
folded, which joke was repeated every week until it became unbearable.

If you are unsure whether a detail is worth writing down, it probably is.
