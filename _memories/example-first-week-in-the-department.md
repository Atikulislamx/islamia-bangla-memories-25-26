The first week is mostly paperwork and corridor geography. You learn which
staircase leads to the honours room, which door is always locked, and which
classmate already knows everyone.

The department itself is quieter than the college ground. There is a shelf of
books that nobody has catalogued, and a corner where the third-year students
sit and talk about literature as though they have already finished reading
everything.

A first memory is rarely dramatic. It is usually one sentence long: *this is
where I will spend the next four years.*

## How a memory is written

A contribution can be as short as a paragraph. What matters is that the details
are true: the room, the day, the smell of the corridor after rain — the small
things that will not survive in a result sheet.

Bengali is welcome, and memories are often strongest in the language they
happened in. For example, a sentence can sit inside an English entry without
being translated:

<p lang="bn">প্রথম দিনে কেউ কাউকে চিনত না, তবু ক্লাসের শেষে সবাই একসঙ্গে চা খেতে গিয়েছিল।</p>

If you want the whole entry to be Bengali, set the language in the entry's
settings and the entire page is rendered with Bengali typography, from the
heading to the caption.

## What happens after you send a memory

1. The memory is read for accuracy, consent, privacy and tone.
2. Anything personal — phone numbers, addresses, roll numbers, results — is
   removed, or the entry is returned to you with a question.
3. The maintainer commits the entry to the repository, and the website rebuilds.

When a real memory replaces this placeholder, the “Sample content” badge
disappears from the card, the archive count changes, and the entry starts
appearing in search results.
